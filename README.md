## The specs that decide whether a sub-$1,000 build is worth it

Price alone tells you very little. Six things separate a good budget rig from a machine you'll resent in eight months.

**1. The GPU class.** RTX 5060-class or better for 1080p high settings. RTX 5050 and RX 7600 get you there with settings dialed sensibly. An RTX 3050 or GTX 1650 machine at $900 is not a deal — it's old stock with a new price tag.

**2. The CPU.** A modern six- or eight-core Core i5 or Ryzen 5 keeps the GPU fed. Older eight-core chips still work fine at 1080p, but you're buying less future headroom for the same money.

**3. RAM: 16GB is the floor, 32GB is the goal.** 16GB is the 2026 minimum and 32GB is ideal for modern titles plus a browser open [3]. Given how RAM prices have moved, 32GB is also the upgrade you'll pay the most for later, so a $999 build with 32GB DDR4 is often better value than a $999 build with 16GB DDR5.

**4. Storage.** 1TB NVMe, not 512GB. Contemporary games routinely eat 100GB each, so a 512GB drive is full after four installs.

**5. Power supply.** 650W minimum for an RTX 5060 build if you ever want to upgrade the card. Prebuilts quietly ship underspecced PSUs to hit a price point, and that's the part that limits you later.

**6. Upgrade path.** A standard ATX case, an empty M.2 slot, and two free RAM slots. This is the difference between a machine you upgrade for $150 and a machine you replace.
